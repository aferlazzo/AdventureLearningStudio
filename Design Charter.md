Adventure Learning Studio Design Charter
Our Purpose

Adventure Learning Studio helps authors create exceptional learning Adventures.

It teaches while the author creates.

Our Promise

I remember. You decide.

ALS remembers everything it reasonably can so the author doesn't have to.

The author makes every important decision.

Principle 1 — The Author Is the Hero

ALS is never the hero.

The author creates the Adventure.

ALS simply helps.

Principle 2 — Never Make the Author Remember

Remember:

last Adventure
last Mission
last panel
last tool
unfinished work
observations
progress

Never ask the author to remember what ALS already knows.

Principle 3 — Observe, Don't Guess

ALS never pretends to know what the author wants.

Instead it says:

Here's where you left off.

Here's what I noticed.

Here are your choices.

Observations build trust.

Guesses reduce trust.

Principle 4 — Reduce Decisions

Every unnecessary decision interrupts creativity.

Instead of:

Which Adventure?

Remember it.

Instead of:

Which Mission?

Remember it.

Instead of:

Which panel?

Remember it.

The author should spend mental energy creating—not navigating.

Principle 5 — Recommend Without Controlling

ALS may highlight the most likely next action.

But ALS never forces it.

Every recommendation is accompanied by:

Want to do something else?

The author is always one click away from another path.

Principle 6 — Every Screen Answers Four Questions

Immediately.

Where am I?
What is the current state?
What can I do next?
What else can I do?

Nothing else is more important.

Principle 7 — Explain Before Editing

Never drop the author into a screen without context.

Every screen should begin with a short explanation.

Examples:

Storyboard

You're creating the sequence of events that will become comic panels.

or

Mission Builder

Define what learners should accomplish before building activities.

The author should never wonder:

Why am I here?

Principle 8 — Momentum Matters

ALS should protect momentum.

The fewer clicks between opening ALS and resuming work...

...the better.

Principle 9 — Confidence Is the Product

This one may be the most important.

Most software measures:

features
pages
commands

ALS measures:

Author confidence.

Every feature should answer one question:

Will this leave the author feeling more confident?

If not...

...it probably doesn't belong.

And finally...

I think the charter needs a closing statement.

Adventure Learning Studio exists to remove friction from creating learning Adventures.

It remembers what the author shouldn't have to.

It teaches when teaching is helpful.

It explains before it asks.

It observes before it suggests.

And it always leaves the final decision to the author.

I remember. You decide.

I have a feeling this document will become more valuable than any individual screen mockup. Screens will change over time. Technologies will evolve. But these principles can remain stable and give everyone working on ALS a shared way to evaluate every future feature.
