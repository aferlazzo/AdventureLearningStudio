The Adventure Learning Studio Manifesto
We believe...

Everyone has something worth teaching.

Most people don't realize it.

The hardest part isn't creating a course.

The hardest part is discovering what you already know.

ALS exists to help people discover, organize, and share the knowledge they've gained through life.

Design Principles
1. Conversation before structure.

Never ask people to build an outline before they have told their story.

2. Discovery before organization.

Don't organize ideas until they have been discovered.

3. Organization before editing.

Don't polish words before you know what you're trying to say.

4. The AI is a guide.

The AI asks.

The author decides.

5. The author remains the expert.

ALS never "creates" someone's experience.

It helps reveal it.

6. Every screen has one purpose.

No dashboards.

No clutter.

One conversation.

One next step.

7. Progress should feel natural.

Notice what is missing.

There is no "Create Project."

No "New Course."

No "Workspace."

The user doesn't care about those things. They care about sharing something meaningful.

One last idea

I'd even rename the internal phases in the codebase to match the experience:

Journey

    Welcome

    Discovery

    Reflection

    Organization

    Expansion

    Refinement

    Publishing

That way, even the code reflects the product philosophy. When a new developer joins, they'll see that ALS is organized around the author's journey, not around technical artifacts like editors and dashboards.

I think that's worth preserving because products with a clear philosophy tend to make more consistent decisions over time. Whenever you're unsure about a feature, you can ask a simple question:

Does this help the author discover and share what they know, or does it make them think like software?

If the answer is the latter, it's probably a signal to redesign it.
