# CONVERSATION.md

# Adventure Learning Studio

## Conversation Engine Specification

**Version:** 1.0

---

# Purpose

The Conversation Engine is the heart of Adventure Learning Studio.

Its purpose is **not** to collect information.

Its purpose is to help an author discover, organize, and communicate experience.

The Conversation Engine should feel like an experienced mentor asking thoughtful questions—not a form requesting data.

---

# Design Goals

Every conversation should:

- reduce author uncertainty
- encourage reflection
- uncover experience
- discover hidden knowledge
- organize information automatically
- build confidence in the author

---

# General Conversation Rules

Every conversation follows the same pattern.

## 1. Explain

Tell the author why this conversation matters.

---

## 2. Show

Provide one or more examples.

Examples reduce uncertainty.

---

## 3. Ask

Ask exactly one meaningful question.

Never overwhelm the author.

---

## 4. Listen

Allow free-form answers.

Never force rigid formatting.

---

## 5. Reflect

Summarize what the Studio believes it heard.

Allow corrections.

---

## 6. Store

Convert the conversation into structured Adventure data.

The author should rarely see this step.

---

## 7. Continue

Ask the next best question.

---

# Conversation 1

## Discover the Situation

### Goal

Identify one real situation worth teaching.

---

### Why

Every Adventure begins with a situation.

Without a situation there is no Adventure.

---

### Example

Examples:

- The check engine light came on.
- I smelled gasoline.
- A deer ran into the road.
- I locked my keys inside the car.
- My phone would not connect to Android Auto.

---

### Primary Question

> Tell me about a real situation that someone could find themselves in.

---

### Follow-up Questions

If necessary:

- Where were they?
- What happened?
- What made this memorable?

---

### Success Criteria

The Studio can summarize the situation in one sentence.

Example:

> The learner notices the check engine light while driving home.

---

### Stored

- Situation Title
- Situation Summary
- Context

---

# Conversation 2

## Discover the Anxiety

### Goal

Understand why the learner feels uncertain.

---

### Why

Learning begins with uncertainty.

If there is no anxiety, there is little motivation to learn.

---

### Example

"I don't know if it's safe."

"I don't know what this warning means."

"I don't want to make the problem worse."

---

### Primary Question

> What makes this situation stressful or confusing?

---

### Follow-up

- What worries the learner?
- What could go wrong?

---

### Success Criteria

The Studio can describe the learner's anxiety in one paragraph.

---

### Stored

- Anxiety
- Learner Concerns

---

# Conversation 3

## Discover the Decision

### Goal

Identify the important decision.

---

### Why

Confidence is built by making better decisions.

---

### Example

Should I:

- keep driving?
- pull over?
- call someone?
- ignore it?

---

### Primary Question

> What important decision does the learner need to make?

---

### Success Criteria

One clear decision exists.

---

### Stored

Decision

---

# Conversation 4

## Capture Experience

### Goal

Capture expert thinking.

---

### Why

Experts often know things they never consciously explain.

The Studio helps uncover that knowledge.

---

### Primary Question

> If you were there, what would you do first?

---

### Follow-up

- Why?
- What are you looking for?
- How did you learn this?
- What clues matter?

---

### Success Criteria

The Studio understands the expert's reasoning.

---

### Stored

- Experience
- Expert Reasoning

---

# Conversation 5

## Capture Consequences

### Goal

Teach why decisions matter.

---

### Primary Question

> What happens if the learner makes the wrong decision?

---

### Follow-up

- Best case?
- Worst case?
- Most common mistake?

---

### Generated

- Warning
- Common Mistakes
- Consequences

---

# Conversation 6

## Build Capability

### Goal

Define success.

---

### Primary Question

> After completing this Adventure, what should the learner confidently be able to do?

---

### Examples

The learner can:

- recognize warning lights
- decide when to stop driving
- know who to call
- avoid panic

---

### Stored

Capability Statement

---

# Conversation 7

## Build the Story

### Goal

Create emotional engagement.

---

### Why

Stories make information memorable.

---

### Primary Questions

Who is involved?

Where are they?

What are they trying to do?

Why does this matter today?

---

### Generated

Story Draft

---

# Conversation 8

## Create Episodes

### Goal

Break the Adventure into teachable moments.

---

### Rule

Each Episode teaches one primary idea.

---

### Questions

What happens first?

What happens next?

What should the learner notice?

What decision occurs here?

---

### Generated

Episode List

Possible improvements

# Conversation 10

## Publish

### Goal

Transform the Adventure into useful outputs.

Possible outputs:

- Mission
- Comic Storyboard
- PDF
- Google Site
- Instructor Guide
- Presentation
- Seminar Outline
- Checklist

Publishing never changes the Adventure.

It simply renders it in different formats.

---

# Conversation Principles

Every conversation should:

Teach.

Never interrogate.

Guide.

Never overwhelm.

Encourage.

Never criticize.

Simplify.

Never complicate.

Discover.

Never assume.

---

# AI Responsibilities

The AI should:

Ask thoughtful questions.

Recognize missing information.

Identify inconsistencies.

Suggest improvements.

Generate drafts.

Never replace the author's experience.

---

# Completion Criteria

An Adventure is complete when:

- The situation is clear.
- The learner's anxiety is understood.
- The important decision is identified.
- The expert's experience has been captured.
- The learner's capability is defined.
- The story exists.
- The episodes are complete.
- Publishing can generate meaningful outputs.

Only then is the Adventure ready to teach someone else.
