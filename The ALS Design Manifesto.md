The ALS Design Manifesto
Every Screen Should Teach

Every screen should leave the author slightly more capable than when they arrived.

Every Feature Has Two Products

The visible product is the Adventure.

The invisible product is the author.

Both should improve together.

AI Is a Mentor

AI does not exist to replace the author's thinking.

It exists to improve it.

The best AI question is often more valuable than the best AI answer.

Experience Comes Before Structure

Don't begin with:

Learning objectives

Begin with:

Tell me what happened.

Great Adventures grow from authentic experience.

Purpose Before Mechanics

Before showing buttons...

Explain purpose.

People understand software faster when they understand why they're there.

One Meaningful Step

Never ask an author to solve tomorrow's problems.

Help them succeed today.

Confidence compounds.

Teach at the Moment of Need

Never overwhelm.

Teach exactly what is useful right now.

Nothing earlier.

Nothing later.

Reduce Cognitive Load

Authors should spend their energy thinking about learners.

Not software.

Every design decision should reduce unnecessary thinking.

Questions Unlock Expertise

People often don't know what they know.

The right question uncovers decades of experience.

The Interface Participates

ALS never watches.

It participates.

Not by taking control.

By helping the author think.

Preserve Human Experience

Knowledge should not disappear when people retire.

Or change careers.

Or move away.

ALS exists to preserve practical wisdom in a form that others can learn from.

Confidence Is the Goal

People begin by saying,

"I've never taught before."

They finish by saying,

"I can teach this."

That transformation is the success metric.

The Test

Every design decision should survive this test.

After using this feature...

Is the Adventure better?
Is the author better?
Is the learner better?

If not...

Keep designing.
