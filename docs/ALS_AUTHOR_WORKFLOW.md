# Adventure Learning Studio Author Workflow

## Purpose

Adventure Learning Studio guides an author from an initial idea to a published Learning Adventure.

The workflow should keep the author focused on the learner and the learning journey rather than on software mechanics.

---

# Workflow

## 1. Create a New Adventure

The author begins by naming the Adventure and briefly describing what it will teach.

## 2. Define the Learning Problem

Describe the real-world problem, situation, or need that makes the Adventure useful.

## 3. Describe the Learner

Identify who the Adventure is for, what they may already know, and what may make the subject difficult or unfamiliar.

## 4. Define the Learning Outcome

Answer:

> What should learners understand, decide, or be able to do when they complete this Adventure?

Confidence may be part of the outcome, but the outcome should be expressed in terms appropriate to the Adventure.

## 5. Create Missions

Break the learning journey into small, focused Missions.

Each Mission should address one useful objective and contribute to the overall outcome.

## 6. Build the Storyboard

Plan how each Mission unfolds.

The storyboard may include situations, characters, scenes, demonstrations, explanations, or other instructional sequences.

## 7. Add Visual Content

Add the visual material that best supports the learning objective.

This may include:

- comic panels
- diagrams
- photographs
- screenshots
- illustrations
- labeled examples

Visual content is encouraged where it improves understanding, but no single format is mandatory.

## 8. Add Instructional Content

Add explanations, dialogue, warnings, tips, examples, questions, and actions needed to complete the Mission.

## 9. Preview

Review the Adventure as the learner will experience it.

Check clarity, sequence, visual presentation, accessibility, and whether the next step is obvious.

## 10. Publish

Generate the selected learning product, such as a website, printed guide, seminar handout, or another supported format.

---

# Guiding Principle

The Studio should guide the author through this process instead of exposing every editing feature at once.

At each stage, the author should be able to answer:

> What should I work on next?
